\begin{abstract}
The Brazilian stock market is subject to pronounced macroeconomic cycles driven by
monetary policy, making it an environment where regime-aware portfolio strategies hold
particular promise.
This paper asks whether an offline reinforcement learning agent trained with a
Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR) reward and macroeconomic state variables produces
portfolios with superior risk-adjusted performance out-of-sample relative to classical
benchmarks on the Brazilian stock exchange (B3).
We formulate portfolio rebalancing as a Markov Decision Process, train a
Conservative Q-Learning (CQL) agent on an offline dataset spanning 2016--2021 whose
state encodes SELIC regime indicators alongside asset-level return dynamics, and
evaluate it on a held-out window from 2022 to 2024 against Equal-Weight,
Minimum-Variance and Risk-Parity portfolios measured on the same 720 trading days.

The answer is negative, and the ablation localizes why.
The full proposed configuration attains a Sharpe ratio of 0.4058 and
CVaR$_{0.95}$ of 0.0260 against 0.6076 and 0.0226 for the equal-weight portfolio,
which dominates it on every metric reported.
Decomposing the model one component at a time, the CQL conservatism penalty
contributes $+0.29$ in Sharpe and is the only component that helps; the CVaR reward
costs $-0.24$ and, contrary to its purpose, worsens realised tail loss at every step
where it is active; the macro state recovers $+0.20$ but leaves the full model below
the simpler Sharpe-reward configuration that omits both.
We trace the reward failure to its construction rather than to the market: the CVaR
signal scores candidate weights against the same twenty-day window the state already
encodes, making it a deterministic function of the observation and therefore no
feedback at all.
Regime-conditional results invert the motivating hypothesis, with the agent's relative
performance worst in tightening phases and positive only while rates fall.
Measured turnover of 38 to 88 times portfolio value per year, against roughly one for
the benchmarks, places plausible transaction costs at the same order of magnitude as
the entire return under comparison.
We report the $1/N$ puzzle as holding in this setting and identify the reward
specification, not the offline paradigm, as the component requiring redesign.

\textbf{Keywords:} offline reinforcement learning; portfolio optimization;
CVaR; conservative Q-learning; Brazilian stock market; regime detection.
\end{abstract}
