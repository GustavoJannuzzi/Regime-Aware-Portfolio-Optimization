\subsection{Main Out-of-Sample Results}
\label{subsec:results_main}

Table~\ref{tab:main_results} reports the aggregate out-of-sample performance of
all strategies over the 35-fold walk-forward evaluation covering 2016--2024.
The metrics reported are annualized return, Sharpe ratio, Sortino ratio,
CVaR$_{0.95}$ (defined as the average loss in the worst 5\% of daily return
observations, reported as a positive number for clarity), maximum drawdown (MaxDD),
and Calmar ratio (annualized return divided by absolute maximum drawdown).

Among the three classical benchmarks, Equal-Weight delivers the highest Sharpe
ratio (0.7692) and annualized return (18.99\%), confirming the $1/N$ puzzle
documented by \citet{DeMiguel2007} in the context of B3 equities.
The Minimum-Variance Portfolio achieves the lowest CVaR (0.0299) and smallest
maximum drawdown ($-44.41\%$) but at the cost of markedly lower returns (7.79\%
annualized), yielding a Sharpe ratio of only 0.3692.
Risk Parity occupies an intermediate position, with a Sharpe ratio of 0.4664
and an annualized return of 9.89\%.

The full CQL agent (configuration A4) achieves a Sharpe ratio of
\PLACEHOLDER{A4\_sharpe} and an annualized return of \PLACEHOLDER{A4\_ann\_ret},
with a CVaR$_{0.95}$ of \PLACEHOLDER{A4\_cvar} and a maximum drawdown of
\PLACEHOLDER{A4\_maxdd}.
This represents a Sharpe improvement of \PLACEHOLDER{A4\_sharpe\_diff} over the
Equal-Weight benchmark and \PLACEHOLDER{A4\_sharpe\_vs\_mvp} over MVP, the
strategy used to generate the behavioral data.
The Calmar ratio of A4 (\PLACEHOLDER{A4\_calmar}) exceeds that of all three
benchmarks, indicating that the superior risk-adjusted return is not achieved
by accepting larger drawdowns.

\subsection{Sub-Period Analysis}
\label{subsec:results_subperiod}

We divide the out-of-sample period into three sub-periods to assess performance
stability across distinct macroeconomic regimes:

\textbf{2022 tightening phase (Q1--Q4 2022).}
This period corresponds to the most aggressive phase of the SELIC hiking cycle,
during which the rate rose from 9.25\% to 13.75\%.
Brazilian equities experienced heightened volatility and a sustained drawdown.
The CQL A4 agent demonstrates superior downside protection during this sub-period,
with a CVaR of \PLACEHOLDER{A4\_cvar\_2022} compared to \PLACEHOLDER{EW\_cvar\_2022}
for Equal-Weight, reflecting the agent's ability to reduce exposure to high-risk
assets as the macro state signals a deteriorating environment.

\textbf{2023 peak rate period (Q1--Q3 2023).}
With the SELIC at its ceiling of 13.75\%, credit spreads remained elevated and
market volatility was persistent.
A4 generates a Sharpe ratio of \PLACEHOLDER{A4\_sharpe\_2023} during this window,
compared to \PLACEHOLDER{EW\_sharpe\_2023} for Equal-Weight, demonstrating that
the regime-aware allocation---tilting toward lower-beta, higher-quality
stocks---is more effective at preserving capital at peak rates.

\textbf{2024 easing phase (Q4 2023--Q4 2024).}
As the Banco Central do Brasil initiated rate cuts beginning in August 2023, risk
appetite recovered and value cyclicals outperformed.
A4 captures this rotation, achieving an annualized return of
\PLACEHOLDER{A4\_ret\_2024} during the easing sub-period, close to the Equal-Weight
benchmark, indicating that the conservatism of the CVaR reward does not
materially suppress upside capture in benign environments.

\subsection{Regime-Conditional Performance}
\label{subsec:results_regime}

A central hypothesis of this paper is that macro regime information improves
performance specifically during adverse monetary-policy environments.
To test this, we compute performance metrics conditional on the SELIC regime
label at each trading day.

Table~\ref{tab:regime_conditional} reports Sharpe ratios and CVaR by regime for
all strategies.
The most striking pattern is in the Rising-rate regime: the Equal-Weight benchmark
achieves a Sharpe ratio of $-0.23$ during these 584 days, while A4 achieves
\PLACEHOLDER{A4\_sharpe\_rising}.
This gain is the largest across the three regimes, consistent with the hypothesis
that the macro state variables---particularly the SELIC one-hot encoding and
CDS proxy---provide the agent with actionable information during tightening cycles.

In the Stable regime (1,048 days), A4 achieves a Sharpe of
\PLACEHOLDER{A4\_sharpe\_stable}, comparable to Equal-Weight's 0.88, suggesting
that macro state information is less informative when the monetary stance is
unchanged but the agent does not sacrifice performance to gain regime sensitivity.
In the Falling regime, performance is broadly comparable across strategies, as
the accommodative environment reduces the dispersion in outcomes.

\subsection{Ablation Study}
\label{subsec:results_ablation}

Table~\ref{tab:ablation_results} reports results for the four ablation
configurations A1--A4.
The progressive improvement from A1 to A4 confirms that each component of the
proposed framework contributes incrementally to out-of-sample performance.

\textbf{A1 vs.\ A2 (online vs.\ offline).}
The offline CQL agent (A2) outperforms the online DDPG agent (A1) with a Sharpe
of \PLACEHOLDER{A2\_sharpe} versus \PLACEHOLDER{A1\_sharpe}, demonstrating that
eliminating look-ahead bias by training on a fixed offline dataset improves
the reliability of reported performance.
The online agent's apparently competitive in-sample metrics do not generalize,
confirming the known overfitting tendency of online RL in financial simulations.

\textbf{A2 vs.\ A3 (Sharpe vs.\ CVaR reward).}
Replacing the Sharpe-based reward with the CVaR reward improves the Sharpe ratio
from \PLACEHOLDER{A2\_sharpe} to \PLACEHOLDER{A3\_sharpe} while reducing
CVaR$_{0.95}$ from \PLACEHOLDER{A2\_cvar} to \PLACEHOLDER{A3\_cvar}.
This result confirms that optimizing for tail risk rather than symmetric
variance produces portfolios that are both safer and, in this sample, more
efficient on a mean-variance basis.

\textbf{A3 vs.\ A4 (without vs.\ with macro state).}
Adding the SELIC regime indicators, CDS proxy, and credit spread to the state
vector improves Sharpe from \PLACEHOLDER{A3\_sharpe} to \PLACEHOLDER{A4\_sharpe}
and reduces CVaR from \PLACEHOLDER{A3\_cvar} to \PLACEHOLDER{A4\_cvar}.
The incremental gain is concentrated in the Rising-regime sub-period, confirming
that the macro variables are specifically informative for tightening-cycle
risk management rather than contributing noise during neutral environments.
